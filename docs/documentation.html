Schema summary

Classes

ClassLabelComment
:ProverbProverbThe surface expression of a proverb: its literal text, language, and image/source domain.
:TacitLessonTacit LessonThe unspoken, language-independent moral a proverb conveys; proverbs from different languages can share the same lesson.
:SituationOfUseSituation of UseThe pragmatic context in which a proverb is deployed: what the speaker uses it to do, such as warn, comfort or encourage.
:SourceDomainSource DomainThe literal imagery/domain a proverb draws on (e.g. birds, fish, sleep, food).
:CrossCulturalEquivalenceCross-Cultural EquivalenceA reified link between two proverbs that share a TacitLesson but diverge (or don't) in imagery. Modeled as a full class so the equivalence itself can carry attributes (type, divergence notes, annotator).
:EquivalenceTypeEquivalence TypeA controlled vocabulary for how two linked proverbs relate, based on whether they share a lesson and whether they share an image. A closed list of four named individuals (below).

Equivalence type vocabulary

IndividualLabelDefinitionExample
:DirectMatchDirect match (same lesson AND same source domain)The two proverbs share both the tacit lesson and the core image (the same scene or object), even if their source-domain tags are worded differently."Chi cerca, trova" (it) and "Kush kërkon, gjen" (sq), both "who seeks, finds"
:PartialOverlapPartial overlapSame lesson; the images share an element or scene type without being the same image.The end of the night (fa) and the end of the storm (it): the same sequence with different scenery
:SameLessonDifferentImageSame lesson, different imageThe two proverbs share a tacit lesson but no element of their imagery.A chain (en) and a wall (fa) that both fail at their weakest point
:FalseFriendFalse friend (same image, different lesson)The two proverbs share an image but teach different lessons."Every cloud has a silver lining" (en) and "A word without a deed is like a cloud without rain" (sq)

Constraints (new in v1.1)

These axioms add no vocabulary. They let a reasoner such as HermiT report data that contradicts the model.

AxiomWhat it saysWhat it catches
Disjoint classesThe six classes are pairwise disjoint.A proverb that is also typed as a :TacitLesson.
Closed list:EquivalenceType is exactly the four individuals above, and the four are declared different from each other.Together with the functional :hasEquivalenceType: a link with two different equivalence types.
Functional properties:hasProverbID, :hasLanguage, :hasText, :linksProverbA, :linksProverbB and :hasEquivalenceType take at most one value.A proverb with two different IDs or two different texts.
Restrictions on :ProverbExactly one :hasLesson to a :TacitLesson; at least one :hasSourceDomain; at least one :usedIn.Together with the disjoint classes: a proverb whose lesson is in fact a source domain.
Restrictions on :CrossCulturalEquivalenceExactly one proverb on each side and exactly one equivalence type.Together with the disjoint classes: a link whose :linksProverbA points to a lesson.

OWL works under the open-world assumption, so these axioms report wrong values but not missing ones: a proverb with no lesson at all is not an inconsistency. Missing values are covered by the SPARQL data checks.
