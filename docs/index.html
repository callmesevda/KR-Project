PROTO

An OWL ontology that models proverbs, the tacit lessons they carry, the situations in which they are used, and the cross-cultural equivalences that link a proverb in one language to a proverb in another when they teach the same unspoken lesson — whether or not they share the same imagery.

Overview

Why PROTO?

Proverbs are one of the clearest cases of tacit knowledge in language: the literal words ("a bird in the hand...") are not the point — the point is the unspoken lesson the phrase is used to teach ("value what you already have over an uncertain gain"). Two cultures can arrive at the same lesson through completely different imagery, or use the very same image to teach something else entirely. PROTO gives that structure a formal, queryable shape: it separates a proverb's surface form from its tacit lesson, its source domain (the literal imagery it draws on), the situation in which it is deployed, and the equivalence relations that connect proverbs across languages.

Ontology

Core classes

The TBox is deliberately small: five core classes plus a controlled vocabulary for equivalence types. Full definitions and properties are in the documentation.

:Proverb

The surface expression of a proverb: its literal text, language, and image/source domain.

Class

:TacitLesson

The unspoken, language-independent moral a proverb conveys.

Class

:SituationOfUse

The pragmatic context in which a proverb would be deployed.

Class

:SourceDomain

The literal imagery/domain a proverb draws on (e.g. birds, fish, sleep, food).

Class

:CrossCulturalEquivalence

A reified link between two proverbs that share a tacit lesson but diverge (or don't) in imagery — modeled as a full class so the equivalence itself can carry attributes.

Class

:EquivalenceType

Controlled vocabulary for how two proverbs relate: same lesson/different image, false friend, partial overlap, or direct match.

Method

How it's built

The knowledge base starts life as a shared spreadsheet ("The Single Source of Truth") where each row is one proverb, annotated with its literal image and tacit lesson and grounded in Wikidata QIDs. A conversion script (xlsx_to_rdf.py) reads the spreadsheet against the TBox and generates the ABox, deduplicating repeated lessons and source domains into shared individuals, and deriving CrossCulturalEquivalence instances automatically for any two proverbs in different languages that share a tacit lesson.
